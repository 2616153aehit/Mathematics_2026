# Exercise 7 — Classifying a Second-Degree Equation

[Exercise sheet](../../../exercises/2_Geometry/03_second_degree_curves_and_surfaces_en.md)

## Problem

Determine the type of object described by:

1. $4x^2+9y^2=36$,
2. $x^2-y^2=1$,
3. $x^2-4y=0$,
4. $x^2+y^2+1=0$.

In the last case, explain why there are no real points.

## Method

Look at the signs of the squared terms and whether one variable appears only linearly. Check whether real points are possible.

## Solution

| Equation | Canonical interpretation | Main features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $4x^2+9y^2=36$ | $x^2/9+y^2/4=1$ | Ellipse centered at the origin, semiaxes $3,2$ |
| $x^2-y^2=1$ | Opposite signs of squared terms | Hyperbola centered at the origin, vertices $(\pm1,0)$ |
| $x^2-4y=0$ | $y=x^2/4$ | Parabola with vertex at the origin, opening upward |
| $x^2+y^2+1=0$ | $x^2+y^2=-1$ | Empty real set |

In the final case, the sum of two real squares is nonnegative and therefore cannot equal $-1$.

## Verification

The first three curves contain $(3,0)$, $(1,0)$, and $(0,0)$, respectively. For the fourth expression, $x^2+y^2+1\ge1$ for all real $x,y$, proving there are no real points.
