# Exercise 2 — Circle by Completing the Square

[Exercise sheet](../../../exercises/2_Geometry/03_second_degree_curves_and_surfaces_en.md)

## Problem

Reduce

$$
x^2+y^2-6x+4y-3=0
$$

to canonical form. Find the center and radius, then substitute two simple points from the circle into both forms of the equation.

## Method

Complete the square separately in the horizontal and vertical variables.

## Solution

Use $x^2-6x=(x-3)^2-9$ and $y^2+4y=(y+2)^2-4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2+y^2-6x+4y-3 &= 0, \\
(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2-16 &= 0.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Canonical form**

$$
(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=16.
$$

The center is $(3,-2)$ and the radius is $4$. Two simple points are $(7,-2)$ and $(-1,-2)$.

## Verification

In canonical form both points give $16+0=16$. In the original equation they give

$$
49+4-42-8-3=0,\qquad 1+4+6-8-3=0.
$$
